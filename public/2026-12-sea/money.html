Connectivity. One eSIM covers the whole trip

Klook's Unlimited eSIM South East Asia covers Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand on one plan, so nobody swaps SIMs at a border. Plans run 1–30 days with tiers from 1GB up to unlimited; the unlimited option is 15GB/day at full speed, then throttled to 1 Mbps. It's an eSIM, so your Philippine number stays active alongside it.

What to actually buy
  • Match the duration to the real trip length, which we can't fix until the return flight is booked. Roughly 11 days as things stand.
  • The ₱32 headline is the 1-day entry tier. An 11-day plan costs meaningfully more; the price only appears once you pick duration and data on Klook.
  • Check each phone supports eSIM before buying. If someone's doesn't, they need a physical SIM instead.
  • Buy the eSIM on Klook : install it on wifi before flying, activate on landing
  • Singapore-only alternatives exist (StarHub, Singtel) but they're the wrong shape for a three-country trip.

How to carry it
  • Bring roughly ₱18,000–20,000 each if you want one number. Under-budgeting in Singapore is the usual mistake. It is roughly twice the daily cost of the other two.
  • Don't change all your pesos at home. Withdraw or pay by card as you go, and keep a small amount of local cash in each country for hawker stalls, night markets and the Padang Besar border, where cards are not reliable.
  • Singapore is nearly all card and QR. Malaysia and Thailand need more cash: street food, small restaurants and taxis often won't take cards.
  • Not included here: accommodation (split five ways), train and flight tickets, Universal Studios, the eSIM, and whatever we do on New Year's Eve. That last one could be anything from free on the riverside to a booked rooftop dinner.
  • Check whether airport terminal fees are already inside the ticket price, and carry a little cash in case they are not.
