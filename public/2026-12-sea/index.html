One-way ticket. Sort this before anything else
  • Singapore and Thailand immigration can both ask for proof of onward travel. With only a one-way booked, the airline may refuse boarding at check-in in Manila/Davao. This gets caught at the counter, not at the border.
  • It's really two proofs: the Shuttle Tebrau (SG→JB) booking shows you'll leave Singapore, and the return flight shows you'll leave Thailand. The CebPac return covers Thailand; book the train early (or hold a refundable throwaway) to cover Singapore.

Open questions

To settle in the next planning session
  • Return flight: an option is now on the table. Cebu Pacific flies Don Mueang → Davao direct, Mon/Wed/Fri, and Mon 4 Jan fits the current last day. Just needs booking. It also fixes onward-travel proof. Note it runs Mon/Wed/Fri only, so the return can't slip by a day: it's Mon 4 or Wed 6 Jan.
  • Kuala Lumpur: one full day or two? The city is settled (JB and Penang dropped). One full day as planned, or a second by flying the last leg to Bangkok instead of the sleeper: see The route. On 31 Dec the flight is NYE-peak (~₱6,000–10,000 pp vs ~₱1,500 for the sleeper), plus an extra KL night.
  • Budget ceiling: what's the per-person number everyone is comfortable with?
  • What the sinking fund is for: spending money, or the group bookings? ₱20,000 each can be one or the other, not both. This decides whether anyone needs to bring extra cash.
  • Whether ₱2,000 a cutoff is enough: ten cutoffs gives ₱20,000 each, which is under the costs we can already price. Raise it, start earlier, or plan to bring the difference.
  • Who holds the fund: one person collecting, and a running note of who has paid in.
  • Pace: packed days or slow ones? Five people rarely agree by default.
  • Christmas Day: anything we want to do on arrival night, or straight to the hotel?
