🎢 The ride plan — do these in this order
  • Rope-drop the two longest queues first. Transformers The Ride and Battlestar Galactica: CYLON consistently hold the longest waits. Be at the gate for opening and hit both before the park fills.
  • Battlestar Galactica: HUMAN vs CYLON (Sci-Fi City) — duelling coasters that launch alongside each other. CYLON is the inverted one, feet dangling, and the more frightening of the pair. HUMAN is the sit-down version. Ride both if the queue allows.
  • Revenge of the Mummy (Ancient Egypt) — indoor coaster in near-total darkness with a backwards launch. Most people's favourite in the park, and it doesn't look like much from outside.
  • TRANSFORMERS The Ride (Sci-Fi City) — 3D motion simulator, the park's signature attraction.
  • Jurassic Park Rapids Adventure (The Lost World) — you will get soaked, not sprinkled. Save it for the afternoon heat and bring a dry bag or poncho for phones.
  • Minion Land — the newest zone, opened Feb 2025 where Madagascar used to be. Despicable Me Minion Mayhem, Silly Swirly, and Buggie Boogie, which is exclusive to Singapore.
  • Puss in Boots' Giant Journey (Far Far Away) — suspended family coaster, gentler, good views over the park.
  • WaterWorld — a live stunt show rather than a ride, with real explosions. Worth timing a sit-down around; check the day's showtimes on arrival. Front rows get drenched.
⚠ Peak week — book ahead
  • Buy dated tickets in advance, not at the gate. Confirm the day's opening hours when booking; they shift seasonally.
  • Price an express/skip-the-queue pass. In the busiest week of the year it may be the difference between six rides and twelve — though for five people it's a real budget call.
  • This gets its own full day deliberately. Don't stack anything else onto it.
  • Monday the 28th would likely be marginally quieter than a Sunday if you'd rather swap the two days. Both are peak regardless.
  • Ride line-ups change — confirm what's open and what's under refurbishment nearer the date.

Connectivity — one eSIM covers the whole trip

Klook's Unlimited eSIM South East Asia covers Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand on one plan, so nobody swaps SIMs at a border. Plans run 1–30 days with tiers from 1GB up to unlimited; the unlimited option is 15GB/day at full speed, then throttled to 1 Mbps. It's an eSIM, so your Philippine number stays active alongside it.

📶 What to actually buy
  • Match the duration to the real trip length — which we can't fix until the return flight is booked. Roughly 11 days as things stand.
  • The ₱32 headline is the 1-day entry tier. An 11-day plan costs meaningfully more; the price only appears once you pick duration and data on Klook.
  • Check each phone supports eSIM before buying — if someone's doesn't, they need a physical SIM instead.
  • Buy before flying and install on wifi. Activate on landing.
  • Singapore-only alternatives exist (StarHub, Singtel) but they're the wrong shape for a three-country trip.

Before we fly

⚠ Three countries, three digital arrival cards — all mandatory
  • 🇸🇬 SG Arrival Card (SGAC) — submit within 72 hours before landing. Airlines check it before boarding, and you show the QR code at immigration.
  • 🇲🇾 Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) — within 3 days before arrival. Mandatory since Jan 2024.
  • 🇹🇭 Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) — up to 72 hours before arrival. Mandatory for foreign nationals since May 2025.
  • These are per person and per country — fifteen submissions across the five of us, each inside its own 3-day window. They can't all be done before we fly. Put reminders in everyone's phone now.
  • All three are free on the official government sites. Don't pay a third-party site to file them.
