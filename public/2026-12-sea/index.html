Do we have to do 16 hours on a train?

All three options happen on Wed 30 Dec. No, the overnight sleeper is the cheapest way and it doubles as a bed for the night, but it is not the only way, and it is the riskiest part of the trip, because it runs once a day and everything depends on catching it.

Why option B is probably the answer
  • It keeps the trip a land journey: we still ride the train the length of Malaysia and walk across the border at Padang Besar. Only the last 1½ hours flies.
  • It buys a whole extra evening and night in Bangkok before New Year's Eve, instead of arriving at 11:00 that same morning off an overnight train.
  • Hat Yai is the airport just inside Thailand, 45–55 minutes from the border station. Fifteen flights a day to Bangkok, from about ₱2,000.
  • The real cost is a Bangkok hotel night the sleeper would have saved us, so call it ₱3,000–4,000 each all in, against roughly ₱1,500 for the train.
  • Whichever we pick, the Thailand Arrival Card is still needed. We enter Thailand by land at Padang Besar in both A and B.

Klook's Unlimited eSIM South East Asia covers Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand on one plan, so nobody swaps SIMs at a border. Plans run 1–30 days with tiers from 1GB up to unlimited; the unlimited option is 15GB/day at full speed, then throttled to 1 Mbps. It's an eSIM, so your Philippine number stays active alongside it.

What to actually buy

How to carry it
  • Bring roughly ₱18,000–20,000 each if you want one number. Under-budgeting in Singapore is the usual mistake. It is roughly twice the daily cost of the other two.

Five of us, so how many rooms is a "booking"?
  • Most hotel rooms sleep two or three, so five people usually means two rooms per city. A serviced apartment or a family room can take all five under one booking.
  • This is the choice that sets the per-person cost, so settle it before comparing prices. Two rooms and one apartment are not comparable numbers.
  • Whatever we pick, it's split five ways. The ledger in Money is where the actual amounts land.
Book Bangkok first
  • We're there over New Year's Eve, the week the city books out and prices climb. It is the one stay where waiting costs real money.
  • Singapore is the urgent one, though: it has to cover the night of Fri 25 Dec, and the property needs telling that we arrive at ~01:30 so the room isn't released.
  • Kuala Lumpur is two nights only because the plan puts us there. Which Malaysian city, and for how long, is still an open question: hold off on a non-refundable rate.

Before we fly

Three countries, three digital arrival cards. All mandatory
  • Singapore Arrival Card: a free online form, submitted within 72 hours before landing. Airlines check it before boarding, and you show the QR code at immigration.
  • Malaysia Digital Arrival Card: the same idea, within 3 days before arrival. Required since January 2024.
  • Thailand Digital Arrival Card: up to 72 hours before arrival. Required for foreign visitors since May 2025.
  • These are per person and per country: fifteen submissions across the five of us, each inside its own 3-day window. They can't all be done before we fly. Put reminders in everyone's phone now.
  • All three are free on the official government sites. Don't pay a third-party site to file them.
